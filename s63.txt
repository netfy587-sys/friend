    <section class="grid-section" id="shift">
      <div class="head">
        <h2>The Shift</h2>
        <span class="idx">01</span>
      </div>
      <div class="col-7">
        <p class="lede" style="color:var(--paper); font-size:1.05rem;">For most of the last century, boys' clothing sat inside a pretty narrow box: blue, grey, sport logos, fitted, done. That box has been slowly cracking open — not because a trend cycle demanded it, but because the people wearing the clothes started asking for something different.</p>
        <p>Pastels, prints, and cuts once filed under "girls' section" now sit comfortably on boys' racks. Oversized fits borrowed from skate and hip-hop culture replaced the snug, sporty silhouette that dominated for decades. And thrifted, worn-in pieces are now a flex, not a compromise.</p>
        <p>None of this happened overnight. It's the result of streetwear going mainstream, a generation of parents less attached to rigid "boy colours," and social platforms where kids see style choices from peers worldwide, not just the mall down the street.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="col-5">
        <div class="stat-block" style="margin-bottom:16px;">
          <span class="big">61%</span>
          <span class="lbl">of Gen Z shoppers say they buy clothing regardless of the gender section it's marketed in, per recent retail survey data</span>
        </div>
        <div class="stat-block">
          <span class="big">2x</span>
          <span class="lbl">growth in resale/thrifted kidswear purchases over the past few years, outpacing new-clothing growth</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="grid-section" id="trends">
      <div class="head">
        <h2>What's Actually Trending</h2>
        <span class="idx">02</span>
      </div>
      <div class="col-12">
        <ul class="trend-list">
          <li>
            <span class="name">Oversized everything</span>
            <span class="desc">Boxy tees, baggy jeans, roomy hoodies — fit has loosened up across nearly every category.</span>
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="name">Colour without apology</span>
            <span class="desc">Lavender, sage, and soft pink now sit next to navy and grey without a second thought.</span>
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="name">Sneaker culture, younger</span>
            <span class="desc">Limited drops and collector mindset have trickled down from adult sneakerheads to ten-year-olds.</span>
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="name">Layering as personality</span>
            <span class="desc">One outfit, three textures — a graphic tee, an open shirt, a jacket tied at the waist.</span>
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="name">Thrifted and proud</span>
            <span class="desc">A worn vintage band tee or a hand-me-down jacket now reads as taste, not necessity.</span>
          </li>
          <li>
            <span class="name">Accessories, finally</span>
            <span class="desc">Crossbody bags, beaded bracelets, and caps have moved from "extra" to expected.</span>
          </li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <blockquote style="padding: 0 56px 40px;">the biggest style shift isn't a specific item — it's boys getting <span>permission</span> to actually have a preference.</blockquote>

    <section class="grid-section" id="buy">
      <div class="head">
        <h2>How To Actually Shop This</h2>
        <span class="idx">04</span>
      </div>
      <div class="col-6">
        <p>You don't need to overhaul a wardrobe to catch up — a few smart swaps do most of the work:</p>
        <ul class="checklist">
          <li><span class="n">01</span><span>Size up one graphic tee or hoodie instead of buying fitted — instant, low-cost update to the silhouette.</span></li>
          <li><span class="n">02</span><span>Add one unexpected colour piece to an otherwise neutral rotation — a sage jacket, a lavender cap.</span></li>
          <li><span class="n">03</span><span>Check a thrift or resale app before buying new sneakers — better pieces, lower cost, less waste.</span></li>
        </ul>
      </div>
      <div class="col-6">
        <ul class="checklist">
          <li><span class="n">04</span><span>Let one accessory do the talking — a bag, bracelet stack, or cap, rather than five competing pieces.</span></li>
          <li><span class="n">05</span><span>Buy for how he actually wants to look, not the section a store has filed it under.</span></li>
          <li><span class="n">06</span><span>Keep one or two "old favourite" pieces in rotation — genuinely worn-in clothes always read better than new.</span></li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="grid-section" id="mistakes">
      <div class="head">
        <h2>What To Skip</h2>
        <span class="idx">05</span>
      </div>
      <div class="col-4">
        <div class="stat-block">
          <span class="lbl" style="color:var(--paper); font-size:0.95rem;">Buying an entire trend-driven wardrobe at once — most of it will feel dated before it's outgrown.</span>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="col-4">
        <div class="stat-block">
          <span class="lbl" style="color:var(--paper); font-size:0.95rem;">Ignoring comfort for a look — a great fit he won't actually move in gets worn once, then shelved.</span>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="col-4">
        <div class="stat-block">
          <span class="lbl" style="color:var(--paper); font-size:0.95rem;">Overriding his own picks constantly — style confidence is built by making some choices himself, wins and misses included.</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </section>

    <section class="grid-section" id="close" style="border-bottom:none;">
      <div class="head">
        <h2>Closing Note</h2>
        <span class="idx">06</span>
      </div>
      <div class="col-7">
        <p style="color:var(--paper); font-size:1.05rem;">What's really shifted here isn't hemlines or sneaker drops — it's the idea that boys get to have an actual point of view about how they look, the same way it's always been assumed girls do. The clothes are just the visible part.</p>
        <p>If you take one thing from this: next time you're shopping together, let him pick the piece that feels most "him," even if it's not what you'd have chosen. That's where real style — not just trend-following — actually starts.</p>
